1. The True Cost of Les Mills: Cumulative Royalties

Les Mills charges clubs on a per-program basis. Offering BodyPump, RPM, and The Trip virtually requires stacking three separate monthly subscriptions. Furthermore, instructor turnover forces operators to pay around $500 per new certification to maintain official teaching status. Over a 3-year period, operating a single studio often exceeds $10,000 in software and training overhead.

2. The Sovereign Alternative: Bobine on Budget Mini PCs

Bobine eliminates recurring costs entirely. Install it in one click on an existing Windows or Mac computer, or on an affordable refurbished mini PC (such as a Dell Wyse 5070 for $45 or an HP EliteDesk 705 for $65), and unlock an enterprise-grade video playout engine. Import standard MP4 or MKV videos, schedule timetable slots with second-precision, and let the system resume automatically after every power cut, with no intervention.

3. 100% Offline Resilience: Never Face Black Screens

Unlike cloud platforms that constantly check DRM rights and freeze during ISP outages, Bobine plays all media directly from the local solid-state drive. The system boots automatically at gym opening, recovers autonomously after blackouts, and operates smoothly even without an active internet connection.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Bobine 100% free while Les Mills Cinema costs €150 to €400 per month?

Bobine is open-source software under the copyleft AGPL-3.0 license. There are no software subscription fees or royalties per screen. You own your standard refurbished hardware (mini PC, HDMI TV).

Can I play custom workout videos and music on Bobine instead of a locked catalog?

Yes. Bobine gives you total freedom to upload your own video files (MP4, MKV 1080p60/4K) and audio courses. There is no locked proprietary catalog or mandatory programming.

How does Bobine guarantee playback during internet outages?

All media files, timetables, and kiosk interfaces run 100% locally from the mini PC's SSD. The system functions entirely offline without any external cloud dependency.

What hardware is required to replace a Les Mills Cinema kiosk?

A standard refurbished x86-64 mini PC (such as Dell Wyse 5070 Celeron J4105 or HP EliteDesk 705 ~€40-€80), 4 to 8 GB RAM, and a standard HDMI screen.
